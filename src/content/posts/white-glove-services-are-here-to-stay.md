High-value transactions are unlikely to be completely outsourced to AI.

Think about real estate. If someone buys a $15M house in Orange County, Manhattan or London, they are unlikely to outsource it to AI. The real estate agent's job here is to handle the transaction, manage the client relationship and make a sale. This doesn't involve a high degree of mathematical intelligence, but it does need salescraft and emotional intelligence. In many cases, real estate agents just make the buyer feel good about their decision. An AI bot can't really do that (unless we are in an AI singularity, and all decisions are being taken by AI).

Similarly, even if private equity firms, enabled by AI, do high-value transactions, they will need top-tier consultants assuring them that their decision makes sense and, in rare cases, stopping them from making a risky one. They will need top lawyers to navigate the legalities, and top bankers to lend them the money.

Beyond private equity-led transactions, CEOs of major companies will still need the rubber stamp of approval of other humans, and help navigating chaos. To those waiting for AI to kill top-tier consulting: don't bet against humans needing white glove reassurance and hand-holding.
