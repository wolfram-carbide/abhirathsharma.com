# Editorial style guide

Use this guide for essays, visual essays and explanatory copy on abhirathsharma.com.

## Voice

- Start with the observation, experience or question that produced the piece when one exists.
- State the argument early. A strong, contestable thesis is preferable to a neutral topic label.
- Write in the first person when the author is part of the story. Do not turn personal judgment into detached report prose.
- Keep the tone conversational and intellectually direct. Address the reader when it helps them follow the chart or argument.
- Preserve distinctive, natural phrasing. Do not replace it with generic editorial polish.

## Structure

- Move from personal trigger to claim, then evidence.
- Explain exclusions and methodological choices in plain language near the point where they matter.
- Let a chart support a claim already introduced in the prose. Do not repeat every visible result below it.
- Use short paragraphs, with one main thought in each.
- Prefer a specific headline that makes the argument over a broad descriptive headline.

## Editing

- Keep the author's level of conviction unless the evidence contradicts it.
- Correct grammar, punctuation and ambiguity without sterilising the voice.
- Use British English: `travelling`, `realised`, `colour`.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Avoid abstract summaries such as “administrative problem” when the author means that scale makes delivery harder.
- Avoid unnecessary qualifications such as “imperfect but useful indication” when the underlying number and its limitation can be stated directly.
- Retain informal bridges such as “As you can see” when they genuinely direct attention to evidence; do not add them mechanically.

## Factual discipline

- Distinguish what the chart shows from what the author infers.
- Do not turn correlation into causation. Population, immigration and HDI can be compared without claiming that one caused another.
- Define comparison groups and averages precisely.
- Keep exact figures consistent between prose, labels, tooltips and source notes.
